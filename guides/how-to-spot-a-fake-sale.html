How to spot a fake sale

Published 23 November 2026 · 6 min read

“Up to 70% off” is the most common line in a fashion newsletter. Sometimes it is a genuine low price. Often it is not: the “was” price was only charged for a few days, the best discounts apply to a handful of odd sizes, or the same item is sold for less somewhere else. Here are the checks we use before calling something a good buy.

1. Ask what the “was” price really was

A discount is only as honest as the price it is compared with. Many stores show a “was” or “compare at” price that the item rarely sold at. The useful question is not “how much is it off?” but “what has this item usually cost over the last two or three months?”

If you can, look up the price history. Price trackers, your own saved screenshots, or simply noting the price when you first see an item all help. If the item sat at the “sale” price for most of the season, the sale is not special.

2. Check whether the discount covers your size and colour

“Up to 70% off” usually means one or two items reach 70%. The headline number often applies to the least popular colour or to sizes that sold slowly. Open the product, choose your size, and look at the price that appears in your basket. That is the only discount that matters to you.

3. Compare the same item at other stores

Brands sell through their own sites and through department stores and marketplaces. The same jacket can be full price at one shop and reduced at another. Search for the exact product name or style code before you buy. If another reputable store has it cheaper without a “sale” banner, the banner was doing the work.

4. Watch out for “made for outlet” lines

Some brands produce items specifically for outlet stores and outlet sections. They can look like mainline pieces but use different materials. A big discount against a mainline price is not meaningful if the product was never sold at that price. Check the product description and fabric composition, not only the name.

5. Count the total, not the tag

For shoppers outside the brand’s home country, the price on the tag is only part of the cost. Add shipping to your forwarder, the forwarder’s fee, any taxes due on import, and local delivery. A 30% discount from a store with free shipping can cost less in total than a 45% discount from a store that charges for delivery. Our guide to buying from US and UK stores in Georgia walks through these costs.

6. Be sceptical of countdown timers

“Ends tonight” banners create urgency, but many sales are extended or repeated. Big seasonal sales (end of season in January and July, Black Friday in November) come back every year. If you are not sure, it is usually safe to wait: a real low price tends to reappear, while a rushed purchase in the wrong size is hard to undo from abroad.

7. Decide before the sale starts

The best protection against fake sales is a short list. Know which pieces your wardrobe actually needs, what you are happy to pay for each, and your size in each brand. When a sale arrives, you only need to check whether one of your items has reached your price. Everything else is noise.

Quick checklist

  • What did this item usually cost over the last 60–90 days?
  • Is my size and colour actually discounted?
  • Is it cheaper elsewhere, without a sale banner?
  • Is it a mainline item or an outlet line?
  • What is the total delivered cost to me?
  • Was this item already on my list?
