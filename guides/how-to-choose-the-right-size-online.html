How to choose the right size online

Published 23 November 2026 · 5 min read

A medium from one brand is a small from another. US, UK and European sizes use different numbers, and the same brand can fit differently from one product line to the next. When returns are slow and expensive, as they are when you buy from abroad, a little preparation saves a lot of money.

1. Measure yourself once, properly

Use a soft tape measure and write down:

  • Chest (or bust): around the fullest part, under the arms.
  • Waist: where you normally wear your trousers, not only your natural waist.
  • Hips: around the fullest part.
  • Inside leg: from the crotch to the floor, without shoes.
  • Foot length: stand on paper, mark heel and longest toe, measure in centimetres. Do it in the evening, when feet are slightly larger.

2. Measure a garment you love

Body measurements tell you what fits your body; garment measurements tell you what fits your taste. Lay a favourite shirt or jacket flat and measure across the chest from armpit to armpit, the length from shoulder to hem, and the sleeve. Many brands publish these “garment measurements” on the product page. Comparing them with your favourite piece is the most reliable check there is.

3. Read the brand’s own size chart, not a generic one

Use the size chart on the brand’s site, and check whether it lists body or garment measurements. If you fall between two sizes, look at the fit description (“slim”, “regular”, “relaxed”) and the fabric: stretch fabrics forgive a smaller size, rigid denim and wool usually do not.

4. Convert sizes carefully

For shoes in particular, US, UK and EU numbers differ, and US women’s and men’s sizes use different scales. Convert using the brand’s chart and your foot length in centimetres rather than a general table.

5. Use reviews for direction, not the answer

Reviews saying “runs small” or “size up” are useful when many people agree. Look for reviewers who mention their height and usual size, and treat a single review with caution.

6. Keep a fit log

Each time something arrives, note the brand, product line, size and how it fits: “Brand A slim shirts, M, perfect” or “Brand B jeans 32, waist tight after washing.” After a few orders this becomes the best sizing tool you have, because it is based on your body and your taste.
