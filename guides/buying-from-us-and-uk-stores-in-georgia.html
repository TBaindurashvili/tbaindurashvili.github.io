Buying from US and UK stores in Georgia

Published 23 November 2026 · 7 min read

Many international fashion stores do not ship to Georgia directly. Shoppers in Tbilisi and elsewhere usually order to a forwarder’s warehouse in the US or UK, and the forwarder flies the parcel to Georgia. It works well, but there are several steps where things go wrong. This guide walks through them in order.

1. Pick a forwarder and get your address

Several Georgian forwarders offer warehouse addresses in the US, the UK, and sometimes Europe and China. When you register, you get a personal address that includes your customer code. Compare a few on:

  • Price per kilogram and the minimum charge per parcel.
  • Flight schedule: how many times a week parcels leave each warehouse.
  • Local delivery: some forwarders deliver to your door, others only to a pick-up office.
  • Support: how easy it is to reach a person when a parcel is missing.

2. Enter the address exactly as given

The most common reason a forwarder “cannot find” a parcel is a missing customer code. Copy every line of the address, including the code, into the store’s checkout. Use the full name on your forwarder account. After you order, save the order confirmation and, once it ships, the tracking number.

3. Track the parcel to the warehouse

Use the store’s tracking number to follow the parcel to the warehouse. When the carrier marks it as delivered, it should appear in your forwarder account within a day or two. If it does not, contact the forwarder with the tracking number, the carrier’s proof of delivery and a screenshot of the address you entered.

4. Declare the parcel

Most forwarders ask you to declare each parcel in your account: what is inside and how much you paid. Upload the invoice or order confirmation if asked. Declare the real price you paid. Incorrect declarations can delay the parcel and lead to penalties.

5. Understand import taxes

At the time of writing, Georgian rules generally let personal parcels below a value threshold (300 GEL) enter without import tax, while parcels above it are subject to VAT (18%) and, for some goods, customs duty. The exact rules, thresholds and how parcels are assessed can change, so check the current guidance from the Revenue Service of Georgia (rs.ge) or ask your forwarder before you order something expensive.

6. Work out the real cost before you buy

A sale price is only part of what you pay. Before you order, add up:

CostWhere it comes from
Item priceThe store, after any discount, in your size
Store shippingTo the forwarder’s warehouse (often free above a minimum)
Sales taxSome US states charge it on delivery to the warehouse
Forwarder feeUsually by weight; shoes and coats weigh more than you think
Import taxesOnly if the rules above apply to your parcel
Local deliveryIf you choose door delivery in Georgia

Two items that both look cheap can end up very differently once weight and delivery are included. Compare totals, not price tags.

7. Plan for returns

Returning an item from Georgia is slow and often costs more than the item is worth. That makes choosing the right size before you buy much more important than for a local purchase. See our guide on choosing the right size online.
